01 · BEng research project · Medical device hardware

Housing and validation of a dual-tuned RF coil for 7 T brain imaging

Deuterium metabolic imaging can show brain-tumour metabolism without radiation, but there was no commercial dual-tuned 1H/2H coil for the Siemens 7 T MAGNETOM Terra.X. I designed and built the mechanical housing and phantom holder, assembled the coil with support from the lab team, tuned and bench-tested it, and checked its safety in electromagnetic simulation.

Design decisions

  • Two concentric cylinders. A 300 mm PMMA inner former carries the birdcage; a 350 mm acrylic outer tube stiffens the assembly and holds the dipole supports. The 20 mm radial gap fits the matching networks and coax routing while staying inside the scanner's bore-access envelope.
  • PLA for the printed parts. Low dielectric constant and negligible RF loss at 300 MHz, dimensionally stable under load, easy to iterate. 20% infill balanced stiffness against print time and mass.
  • Modular by necessity. The full diameter would not fit the Ultimaker's build volume, so I split the cradle into smaller parts fastened together.
  • Built to be serviced. Reusable PMMA former and serviceable matching networks, so parts can be retuned or replaced instead of rebuilding the coil.

Testing and validation

I bench-tuned all eight proton dipoles and both birdcage ports under phantom load and measured the full S-matrix on a VNA. Every dipole met the −12 dB return-loss criterion. The birdcage resonated at 45.2 MHz with −18.6 / −15.8 dB reflection and −29.4 dB isolation.

I then built FDTD models of the coil with both phantoms and the "Duke" virtual human head, checked them against the bench data, and mapped B1+ field and SAR. The phantom holder mattered here: any offset from isocentre changes the coupling, so reproducible positioning was a precondition for a fair bench-to-simulation comparison.

What I'd change next time

The acrylic housing pulled the dipole resonance down by about 2 MHz, which I compensated for by tuning on the bench to 299.2 MHz. In a second revision I would design that shift in when selecting components, removing a retuning step. The next mechanical step is head-specific ergonomics: a head-shaped phantom and positioning fixtures so subjects sit at the same spot every session.

02 · Group design project · Embedded device

BreatheSafe: an indoor air monitor for people with asthma

Indoor particulate levels can exceed outdoor ones after cooking or cleaning, and you can't see it happen. We built a low-cost desk device that measures temperature, humidity, PM2.5 and PM10, tells the user at a glance whether the room is fine, and logs everything for later.

Designing around the user

  • Glance, then read. An RGB LED gives the overall verdict from across the room. The 16×2 LCD gives the detail when you walk over.
  • One control. A joystick cycles through five screens and wraps back to the start, so there is no menu to get lost in.
  • Alarms that mean something. The buzzer sounds only when every parameter is BAD, so it isn't ignored after a week.
  • Data you can take away. Readings go to a CSV on microSD that opens straight in Excel, for spotting patterns or sharing with a clinician.
  • Fails gracefully. If the SD card is missing or full, live readings keep working and only logging stops.

03 · Individual coursework · Fluid mechanics

Finite element model of blood flow in a branching vessel network

How much of the flow does each branch get, and what pressure does it take to drive it? I wrote a MATLAB finite element solver for steady, incompressible Navier–Stokes flow through a 2D arterial tree to answer both.

How it works

Blood is treated as a Newtonian fluid. The solver assembles the weak form on triangular Taylor–Hood elements, which satisfy the inf–sup condition without stabilisation, and solves the fully coupled velocity–pressure system monolithically with Newton–Raphson. Boundary conditions are a prescribed inlet velocity, no-slip walls and traction-free outlets. I refined the mesh three times to confirm the answer stopped changing.

What I learned from it

The numbers converged cleanly, but the flow split was lopsided because traction-free outlets let geometry alone decide where the flow goes. Real vessel beds push back. The fix is resistance or Windkessel outlet conditions. Getting boundary conditions right matters as much as getting the solver right, and that applies just as much to a microfluidic channel as to an artery.

04 · Object-oriented programming coursework · Software

Cardiac MRI perfusion analysis in C++

A command-line tool that loads a time series of cardiac MRI frames, tracks contrast agent through the left-ventricular blood pool, and reports when it arrives, when it peaks and how fast it is taken up, with plots drawn in the terminal.

What it computes

  • Mean signal S(d) inside a 5×5 pixel mask on the blood pool, for every frame.
  • Peak frame and signal, plus the peak image rendered in the terminal.
  • Contrast arrival: the first frame where S(d+1) − S(d) exceeds 10.
  • Uptake gradient G = (Speak − Sarrival) / (dpeak − darrival).

How it's structured

I kept file loading, masking and analysis in separate modules. An abstract Mask class lets a new region shape be added by overriding one method. PerfusionImageDataset extends a general TemporalDataset, so the time-series handling can be reused for other kinds of scan. Encapsulated image data, input validation and a verbose debug mode make failures easy to trace.
